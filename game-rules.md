# Make‑a‑Million – Official Rules Reference

**Make‑a‑Million** is a partnership trick‑taking card game (Parker Brothers, 1934). Teams compete to capture valuable *Money cards* in tricks and be the first side to reach \$1,000,000.

## Components and Card Hierarchy

- **Deck:** 55 custom cards in one deck: 52 *suit cards* (4 suits × 13 ranks) plus 3 special trumps (Tiger, Bull, Bear).  
- **Suits & Ranks:** Four suits (Red, Yellow, Black, Green). Each suit’s cards rank (highest→lowest): \$40,000; \$30,000; \$15,000; 11; \$10,000; 9; 8; 7; \$5,000; 4; 3; 2; 1. (*Note:* There is **no** 6.).  
- **Money vs. Number Cards:** Cards with a dollar value (\$40k, \$30k, \$15k, \$10k, \$5k) are **Money cards**; the others (11, 9, 8, 7, 4, 3, 2, 1) are plain **number cards**. Only Money cards contribute to the score.  
- **Special Cards:**  
  - **Tiger** – the highest trump in the game.  
  - **Bull** – doubles trick value.  
  - **Bear** – cancels trick value.  

## Setup and Partnerships

- **Partnerships:** 4 players form two fixed teams of two (partners sit opposite each other). 
- **Dealer:** Passes clockwise each hand. The dealer shuffles and deals **all** cards, one by one. After dealing, 3 cards remain – these form the hidden **widow** (also called the nest).  
- **Misdeal:** Any player with \$15,000 or less in hand may call for a re-deal. (This isn't technically an official rule but is an extremely common **house rule**)

## Bidding (Auction)

- **Purpose:** Players bid for the privilege of choosing trumps and taking the widow.  
- **Opening Bid:** Typically starts around \$175,000 (spoken “one-seventy-five”). Bids between \$175,000 and \$200,000 are common opening bids. 
- **Turn Order:** Bidding begins with the player to the **left of the dealer**, who **must give an opening bid**, and proceeds clockwise. Each player thereafter (including the opening player once the turn comes back to him) will either **bid** or **pass**. (If a player passes, they cannot bid again this hand.) 
- **Increments:** Each new bid must exceed the previous by at least \$10,000. Some common house rules dictate \$5,000 as the bid increment.
- **Ending the Auction:** Continue until all but one player have passed. The remaining player is the *highest bidder*. That bid amount becomes the team’s contract; The team that took the bid aims to earn at least that much, and the opposing team aims to "set" them.

## Widow Pickup and Trump Naming

- The highest bidder now picks up the 3-card widow into their hand. They may *keep* any or all of these cards, then must **discard** 3  cards (to return to the original hand size).  
- **Discard restrictions:** The bidder **must not discard a Money card if they have any plain number card to discard**. (If the bidder has fewer than 3 number cards, they discard as many number cards as possible, and must then also discard Money card(s) to reach 3 total.) Under no circumstances may the Bull, Bear, or Tiger be discarded.  
- After discarding, the highest bidder **names trump** (one of the four colors) for this hand. Only the bidder sees the widow cards.

## Trick-Taking Play

- **Lead:** The highest bidder leads the first trick by playing any card from hand (face-up). Play proceeds clockwise.  
- **Following suit:** Each player must **follow the lead color** if able. If a player has no card of the led color, they may **discard** (play any other color number card) or **play a trump**. If void in lead color, a player may also play the Bull or Bear (if holding one), or even a Money card (hoping a partner will win the trick).  
- **Restrictions on Leads:** A player **cannot lead** with the Bull or Bear if they hold any other card (number, Money, or Tiger). Bulls and Bears may only be led when they are the player’s last card(s).  
- **Winning the trick:** The trick is won by the **highest card of the suit led**, unless one or more trumps were played. If trumps appear, the highest trump wins. (The Tiger ranks above all trumps.)
- **Collecting tricks:** The winner of the trick takes the cards and places them face-down in front of them (or their team). Partners’ tricks are kept together. Conventionally, tricks containing a Bull or Bear are kept separate or marked, since their scoring effect differs. The winner of each trick leads the next.

## Special Cards

- **Bull:** Can only be played when **void in lead suit** (or as last card). If played, it *doubles* the total value of Money cards in that trick for the team that captures it. (Example: a trick with \$40k and a Bull yields \$80k.) 
- **Bear:** Same play conditions as the Bull (only when void or last card). When captured, it *nullifies* all Money in that trick (zero points). If both Bull and Bear are played in one trick, only the **last-played** one has effect. (E.g., Bear after Bull cancels instead of doubling.)  
- **Tiger:** The most powerful card, winning over all other cards (even the \$40k trump). It is considered to be the same suit as trump; If led, it compels others to follow with trump if possible; If trumps are led and the Tiger-holder has no other trumps, they must play the Tiger.

## Scoring (Hand Result)

- After all cards are played, each partnership totals the values of *captured Money cards*.  
- Any money in a trick where the Bull was the last special card is counted twice.
- Any money in a trick where the Bear was the last special card is canceled to zero.
- **If the bidders made their bid** (captured ≥ bid value), they **score** the full total they captured. If they **failed to make the bid**, they are “set back”: they score *nothing* and must subtract the bid amount from their cumulative score (including going negative if necessary). The opposing team (the setters) simply score what they captured.  
- The game continues with cumulative totals. The **first team** to reach \$1,000,000 wins. If both teams exceed \$1,000,000 on the same hand, the **higher** score wins. (No extra tiebreaker hand is played.)

## Implementation Notes

- **Raise Increment ($5k vs $10k):** All official text uses \$10,000 steps. Any \$5k rule is a non‑standard but common variant.
- **Simultaneous Win:** There are no official rules addressing the rare case in which both sides exceed \$1,000,000 on the same hand *and* end up with the same score. House rules might call for a tiebreaker hand or might call the game a tie.
