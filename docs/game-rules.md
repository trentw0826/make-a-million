# Make‑a‑Million – Official Rules Reference

**Make‑a‑Million** is a partnership trick‑taking card game (Parker Brothers, 1934). Teams compete to capture valuable *Money cards* in tricks and be the first side to reach \$1,000,000.

## Components and Card Hierarchy

- **Deck:** 55 custom cards in one deck: 52 *suit cards* (4 suits × 13 ranks) plus 3 special trumps (Tiger, Bull, Bear).  
- **Suits & Ranks:** Four suits (Red, Yellow, Black, Green). Each suit’s cards rank (highest→lowest): \$40,000; \$30,000; \$15,000; 11; \$10,000; 9; 8; 7; \$5,000; 4; 3; 2; 1. (*Note:* There is **no** 6.).  
- **Money vs. Number Cards:** Cards with a dollar value (\$40k, \$30k, \$15k, \$10k, \$5k) are **Money cards**; the others (11, 9, 8, 7, 4, 3, 2, 1) are plain **number cards**. Only Money cards contribute to the score.  
- **Special Cards:**  
  - **Tiger** – the highest trump in the game.  
  - **Bull** – doubles trick value.  
  - **Bear** – cancels trick value.  

## Setup and Partnerships

- **Partnerships:** 4 players form two fixed teams of two (partners sit opposite each other). 
- **Dealer:** Passes clockwise each hand. The dealer shuffles and deals **all** cards, one by one. After dealing, 3 cards remain – these form the hidden **widow** (also called the nest).  
- **Misdeal:** Any player dealt money totalling \$15,000 or less is may call for a re-deal (called a "misdeal"). A misdeal is called on the player's first turn in the auction, in place of a bid or pass; once a player has bid or passed they can no longer call one. Bids already made are void, and the same dealer repeats the deal. The only condition for calling a misdeal is the \$15,000 condition (having special cards or having already called multiple other misdeals does not matter)

## Bidding (Auction)

- **Purpose:** Players bid for the privilege of choosing trumps and taking the widow.  
- **Opening Bid:** Minimum bid is  \$175,000 (spoken “one-seventy-five”). Bids between \$175,000 and \$200,000 are common opening bids. 
- **Turn Order:** Bidding begins with the player to the **left of the dealer**, who **must give an opening bid**, and proceeds clockwise. Each player thereafter (including the opening player once the turn comes back to him) will either **bid** or **pass**. (If a player passes, they cannot bid again this hand.) 
- **Increments:** Each new bid must exceed the previous by at least \$10,000. Some common house rules dictate \$5,000 as the bid increment. There is **no maximum bid**.
- **Ending the Auction:** Continue until all but one player have passed. The remaining player is the *highest bidder*. That bid amount becomes the team’s contract; The team that took the bid aims to earn at least that much, and the opposing team aims to "set" them.

## Widow Pickup and Trump Naming

- The highest bidder now picks up the 3-card widow into their hand. They may *keep* any or all of these cards, then must **discard** 3  cards (to return to the original hand size). 
- The discarded widow cards are kept **hidden and unused** for the remainder of the round, even if they are money cards.
- **Discard restrictions:** The bidder **must not discard a Money card if they have any plain number card to discard**. (If the bidder has fewer than 3 number cards, they discard as many number cards as possible, and must then also discard Money card(s) to reach 3 total.) Under no circumstances may the Bull, Bear, or Tiger be discarded.  
- After discarding, the highest bidder **names trump** (one of the four colors) for this hand. Only the bidder sees the widow cards.

## Trick-Taking Play

- **Lead:** The **player to the left** of highest bidder leads the first trick by playing any card from hand. Play proceeds clockwise.  
- **Following suit:** Each player must **follow the lead color** if able. If a player has no card of the led color, they may play any card in their hand.
- **Restrictions on Leads:** A player **cannot lead** with the Bull or Bear if they hold any other card (number, Money, or Tiger). Bulls and Bears may only be led when they are the player’s last card(s). The first card of the trick that isn't the Bull or Bear determines the suit to be followed by the remaining players. Until a colored card is played, the trick has no suit and any card may be played.
- **Winning the trick:** The trick is won by the **highest card of the suit led**, unless one or more trumps were played. If trumps appear, the highest trump wins. (The Tiger ranks above all trumps.)
- **Collecting tricks:** The winner of the trick takes the cards and places them face-down in front of them (or their team). Partners’ tricks are kept together. Conventionally, tricks containing a Bull or Bear are kept separate or marked, since their scoring effect differs. The winner of each trick leads the next.

## Special Cards

- **Bull:** Can only be played if the trick suit hasn't been established or the player is out of the trick suit. If played, it *doubles* the total value of Money cards in that trick for the team that captures it. (Example: a trick with \$40k and a Bull yields \$80k.) 
- **Bear:** Same play conditions as the Bull (only when the trick suit hasn't been established or the player is out of the trick suit). When captured, it *nullifies* all Money in that trick (zero points). If both Bull and Bear are played in one trick, only the **last-played** one has effect. (E.g., Bear after Bull cancels instead of doubling.)  
- **Tiger:** The most powerful card, winning over all other cards. It is considered to be the same suit as trump, meaning if it's led, it compels others to follow with trump if possible; If trumps are led and the Tiger-holder has no other trumps, they must play the Tiger. The Tiger **cannot be played out of suit**; if a non-trump suit is led and the Tiger-holder is not void in that suit, they cannot play the Tiger.

## Scoring (Hand Result)

- After all cards are played, each partnership totals the values of *captured Money cards*.  
- Any money in a trick where the Bull was the last special card is counted twice.
- Any money in a trick where the Bear was the last special card is canceled to zero.
- **If the bidders made their bid** (captured ≥ bid value), they **score** the full total they captured. If they **failed to make the bid**, they are “set back”: they score *nothing* and must subtract the bid amount from their cumulative score (including going negative if necessary). The opposing team (the setters) simply score what they captured.  
- The game continues with cumulative totals. The **first team** to reach \$1,000,000 wins. If both teams reach  \$1,000,000 on the same hand, the **higher** score wins. (No extra tiebreaker hand is played.)

## Table Visibility

The application reproduces the **physical tabletop experience**. A player sees exactly what they would see sitting at a real table, and nothing more:

- **Own hand.** Other players' hands are never shown, only how many cards each player holds.
- **The trick in progress:** the cards played so far in the current trick, who played them, and the trick suit. Once a trick is taken, its cards are turned face‑down and are no longer visible.
- **The auction:** bids and passes as they are spoken, and the resulting contract (bidder, amount) and named trump.
- **Trick piles:** how many tricks each player has taken this hand (each player gathers the tricks they win into a face‑down pile in front of them). The money inside those piles is **not** shown.
- **The score sheet:** cumulative scores, and for each completed hand the bidder, bid, trump, whether the bid was made or set, and each side's result.
- **Hand result:** when the last trick is taken, both sides' captured Money is counted openly, so the hand total and the cards that produced it are shown at that point only.

There is **no** record of cards played in earlier tricks and **no** running money total during a hand. Players who want that information must remember it, just as at the table. (Bots are held to the same information limits; see `bot-standards.md`.)

**Solo analysis.** A solo game (one player and three bots; see `multiplayer.md`) can turn on a training aid. With hints, it shows a bar with your side's chance of winning and the value of each of your choices before you choose, and grades each move after it; with feedback only, it just grades each move after you make it. It knows exactly what a bot in your seat would know, which is everything your seat could legitimately know, including the earlier tricks you would otherwise have to remember (see *Analysis* in `bot-standards.md`). It never reveals another player's cards. It is offered only in solo games, where there is nobody else at the table to gain an edge over.

## Other Notes

- **Misdeal calls:** In the application, bidding begins as soon as the cards are dealt. A player whose Money totals \$15,000 or less is offered "Call misdeal" alongside bidding and passing on their first turn in the auction, and only then; nobody else sees the offer. A call re‑deals immediately with the same dealer.
- **Raise Increment ($5k vs $10k):** All official text uses \$10,000 steps. Any \$5k rule is a non‑standard but common variant.
- **Simultaneous Win:** There are no official rules addressing the rare case in which both sides reach   \$1,000,000  *and* end up with the same score in one hand. In this case, let the bidding team win.
- **Table talk:** Any verbal or non-verbal communication between partners that conveys information about a player's hand, strategy, or intent during active play is strictly forbidden.
